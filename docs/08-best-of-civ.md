# 08 — Best of Civ 5, 6 and 7

Civius Maximus doesn't copy any one game. For each system it takes the version that plays best,
and it's especially tuned for **up to 8 friends taking simultaneous turns**. That last point
matters: anything that adds micromanagement makes *everyone* wait for the slowest player, so
when two versions are equally deep, we pick the one that's faster to play.

Legend: **5** = Civ V (with Gods & Kings / Brave New World), **6** = Civ VI (with Rise & Fall /
Gathering Storm), **7** = Civ VII.

## The picks

### Map and movement

| System | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hex grid, one military unit per tile | **5 / 6** | Makes positioning and terrain matter. No stacks of doom. |
| Moving with too few movement points | **5** | You can always move 1 tile if you have any MP left. Civ 6's rule leaves units stuck in front of hills, which feels bad. |
| Rivers on tile edges, river crossing penalty | **6** | Rivers become meaningful borders and defensive lines. |
| **Navigable rivers** (boats can sail up big rivers) | **7** | Inland cities become naval targets and trade hubs. It adds variety to maps. |
| Mountains impassable, tunnels late game | **6** | Natural chokepoints are great for multiplayer wars. |

### Leaders and civs

| System | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Leaders separate from civs** (any leader can lead any civ) | **7** | Perfect for "add any famous leader we want": a leader is one file, and they can lead any civ ("Napoleon leading the Mongols"). The civ picker starts on the leader's historical civ. |
| Changing civs at each age (Civ 7's age transitions) | **7, optional** | Divisive among players, so it's an *optional* "Ages mode" module, off by default. |
| AI agendas (visible likes/dislikes) | **6** | AI behavior becomes readable and moddable. |

### Cities and growth

| System | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Towns vs Cities** | **7** | New settlements start as *Towns*: no build queue, their production becomes gold, and you pick one focus (farming, mining, trade, fort...). You upgrade the important ones to *Cities* with gold. Wide empires stay manageable, and simultaneous turns stay fast with 8 players. |
| **Growth develops tiles** (no builder units) | **7** | Each time a settlement grows, you pick a tile to develop, and the right improvement goes on it automatically (farm on grassland, mine on hills...). It's the same decision Civ 6 builders gave you, without moving workers around every turn. |
| **Districts with adjacency bonuses** | **6** | Civ 6's best idea: city planning as a puzzle, with each city shaped by its terrain. Kept nearly as-is. |
| **Happiness as a yield + Celebrations** | **7** | Replaces Civ 6's housing *and* amenities with one clear stat. Surplus happiness builds toward a **Celebration** (pick a temporary bonus policy slot). A deficit slows growth and yields. |
| **Settlement limit** (soft cap on settlements before happiness penalties) | **7** (spirit of **5**'s global happiness) | Keeps the "wide vs tall" balance from Civ 5 without punishing expansion too hard. The limit grows with techs and civics. |
| Workable tiles up to 3 rings, buy tiles with gold | **5 / 6** | Familiar and works well. |
| **Puppet** captured cities | **5** | When you conquer a city you can puppet it: it runs itself, gives you yields, and adds no micromanagement. Great for conquest-heavy players in 8-player games. Civ 6 removed this. |

### Science, culture and government

| System | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Separate tech tree (science) and civics tree (culture) | **6** | Culture-focused players get their own progression path. |
| **Boosts** (Eurekas / Inspirations): do X, get 40% of a tech | **6** | Rewards playing to your situation. Each game's research path differs. |
| **Governments + policy cards** | **6** | Huge strategic flexibility, very moddable, and easy to understand. |
| Celebrations add a temporary policy slot | **7** | Ties happiness into the policy system nicely. |
| **Ideologies with pressure** (late-game governments affect rival citizens via tourism) | **5** | Creates great late-game tension between friends: "your people want my ideology". Built into the tourism module. |

### Military

| System | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Combat formula (exponential strength difference, 100 HP) | **6 / 7** | Readable, with a combat preview. Strength differences matter but don't decide everything. |
| **Commanders** (a unit that packs up to 4 units, moves them as one, gains promotions and gives area bonuses) | **7** | Keeps one-unit-per-tile tactics but removes late-game micromanagement. It replaces Civ 6's corps/armies. Big win for simultaneous turns. |
| Promotion trees, promotion heals the unit | **6** | Promoting becomes a real tactical decision. |
| Support units (battering ram, siege tower, medic) | **6** | Adds siege tactics. |
| Cities defend themselves (ranged strike), walls | **5 / 6** | Early rushes can be survived, and sieges need planning. |
| **War Support** | **7** | Replaces Civ 6's war weariness and grievances with one number per war. A surprise war gives the *defender* war support, and losing war support costs happiness. Simple to understand and hard to abuse. |

### Diplomacy and the world

| System | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Influence as a yield** (spent on diplomatic actions, deals, befriending independents) | **7** | Diplomacy gets a real economy instead of being free button presses. |
| **Enforced deals + public promises** | ours | For friends specifically: signed terms are enforced by the game, chat is just talk. |
| **Independent Powers** (barbarians and city-states merged: hostile camps can be befriended with influence and grow into city-states) | **7** | The map is alive early, and players compete for neutrals without fighting each other. |
| Envoys and suzerain bonuses | **6** | Civ 6's best city-state system. Bonuses level up as you invest. |
| City-state quests | **5** | Gives clear goals for earning influence with them. |
| Trade routes that build roads | **6** | The road network grows on its own. Fewer things to manage. |

### Great people, religion, eras

| System | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Named great people** with unique abilities, earned in a world-wide race | **6** | Civ 7 cut most of this. It's a great hook for custom characters (your friends as great people). |
| Religion: beliefs, pressure, missionaries, no theological combat by default | **5** feel, **6** holy sites | Civ 5's religion was smoother. Civ 6's religious combat is tedious, so it's an optional toggle. |
| **Golden / Dark / Heroic ages** with dedications | **6** | Comeback mechanics: a Dark Age gives access to powerful special policies. |
| **Legacy goals** per era (military, science, culture, economic: four visible goals that earn era points) | **7** | Clear direction for each era, without Civ 7's hard age resets. |
| Governors | **6** | Lets you specialize cities. Optional module. |
| Crises at the end of an era | **7, optional** | Fun chaos for groups who want it. Off by default. |

### Victories and pacing

| System | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Classic victories: domination, science, culture, religious, score | **5 / 6** | Clear end goals everyone understands. Civ 7's age-based legacy victories are available through the optional Ages mode. |
| Culture victory with domestic vs foreign tourists | **6** | Clearer than Civ 5's influence levels, though both are similar. |
| Simultaneous turns with an End Turn button | all | Everyone plays at once. The turn ends when everyone has clicked done (or the timer runs out). |
| Dynamic / hybrid turns as an option | **5 / 6** | Players at war alternate turns so battles aren't a click race. Off by default. |

### Interface

| System | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Lenses (yield, settler, appeal, religion...) | **6** | Planning without spreadsheets. |
| **Strategic view** (flat icon map) | **5** | Fast late-game play, and perfect for large 8-player maps. |
| Clean, information-dense panels | **5** | Civ 5's UI was the clearest of the three. |
| Number breakdowns in tooltips | ours | Every number can be clicked to see why it is that number. |

## What we deliberately dropped

| Dropped | From | Replaced by | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Builder units | 6 | Growth develops tiles (7) | Busywork that slows simultaneous turns |
| Housing + amenities | 6 | Happiness yield + celebrations (7) | Two overlapping stats. One is clearer |
| Corps / armies | 6 | Commanders (7) | Commanders do the same job with more depth and less clicking |
| War weariness + grievances | 6 | War support (7) | Simpler and fairer |
| Forced civ switching each age | 7 | Optional Ages mode | Many players dislike it |
| Strict movement rule | 6 | Civ 5 rule | Frustrating |
| Global happiness | 5 | Settlement limit (7) | Same tall/wide balance without hard punishment |
| Theological combat | 6 | Optional toggle | Tedious for most groups |
| Chopping woods for production (builders) | 6 | "Clear land" city project | Keeps the option without the builder |

Every row above is a starting point. If a pick feels wrong in playtesting, swap it. Most of these are
data or module changes, not rewrites.
